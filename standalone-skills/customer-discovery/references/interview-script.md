# Interview Script

Adapt this script to the study's hypotheses before the first conversation. Keep the order; drop a question only when no hypothesis needs it.
Ask one question at a time and wait out the full answer.
Capture behavior in the participant's own words.

Each question below carries a note on what it tests and a note on what not to ask instead.
A future-intent answer is not a substitute for the behavioral question. "Would you use it?" gets polite agreement, not evidence.
Do not anchor on a price or a number before the participant states their own.

## Opening

- Introduce yourself, the purpose and how the session uses what is said.
- State the duration and ask for consent to continue.
- This tests nothing. It records permission and the honest scope of the session.
- Do not describe the product idea here; an opening pitch produces agreement, not history.

## Warm-Up

- "Walk me through your role and what a normal week looks like."
- Tests: the participant's context and their vocabulary for the problem space.
- Do not steer the answer toward the study's target problem yet.

## Recent Event Walk-Through

- "Think about the last time you dealt with <problem task>. Walk me through that day, step by step."
- Follow with "What happened just before that?" and "What did you do next?"
- Tests: the real workflow, its trigger and its steps, as it actually happened.
- Do not accept a general description; ask again for one specific recent event.

## Current Alternative

- "What do you use today for this? Describe the last time you used it."
- "What is the most annoying part of that?"
- Tests: the current alternative, its workaround and its real cost.
- Do not ask whether the participant likes a product idea; ask what they already do.

## Cost And Frequency

- "How often does this come up? When was the time before this one?"
- "What did that cost you — time, money or something else?"
- Tests: frequency and the problem's cost in the participant's own words.
- Do not suggest a number or a unit; wait for theirs.

## Switch Trigger

- "What would have to happen for you to change how you handle this?"
- Tests: the event that would make the participant actually switch.
- Do not pitch the product as that change; record the trigger they name.

## Tried And Abandoned

- "Have you tried anything else for this? What happened?"
- Tests: prior attempts, including abandoned tools and home-made workarounds.
- Do not argue with a past choice; record why it stopped.

## Closing

- "Who else deals with this? Would you introduce me?"
- "May I follow up if something is unclear?"
- Thank the participant and restate how their notes are stored.
- Do not close with a pitch or a future-intent question.

## Note Rules

- Write notes during the session or right after it.
- Redact names, employers and contact details before any note leaves the session.
- One participant gets one code. The code maps to contact details only outside the repository.
- Record exact quotes for the strongest statements; summarize the rest.
- Record the date and the session length with every set of notes.
