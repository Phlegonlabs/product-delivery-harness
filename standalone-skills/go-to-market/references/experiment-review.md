# Experiment And Outcome Review

Use this guide when preparing an experiment or reviewing a completed measurement window.

## Define The Test

Record the audience, channel/account, content angle, intended action, destination, owner, effort limit and proposed window.
Use explicit dates and the campaign timezone when known.
Separate content, audience and destination hypotheses. Change one major uncertainty when practical.

Define the primary outcome from the project's goal: qualified conversations, waitlist signups, activated trials, useful installations or returning usage.
Keep impressions, reactions, followers and clicks as supporting observations unless one is the accepted goal.
Channel engagement is not a substitute for adoption or retention.

Set a decision rule suited to baseline volume, elapsed time and production cost.
Allow continuing, revising, stopping or collecting more evidence.
Do not invent conversion benchmarks, sample sizes or cost limits without a stated basis.
Sparse data can be inconclusive; name the additional observation needed.

## Connect Measurement

Reuse existing campaign naming and metric definitions.
When available, cite the exact PRD metric, page/operation ID and applicable Activation `MS-*` source in the campaign record.
Retain their existing definitions, release scope and source status instead of copying or redefining them.
Missing product records or unverified measurement sources do not block strategy or draft preparation; state the gap.
For applicable external website links, propose source, medium, campaign and content identifiers without personal information.
Preserve query parameters, fragments, signed URLs and destination behavior; omit unsafe tags and explain the gap.
Do not tag internal navigation merely to manufacture attribution.

[Google's URL guide](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952?hl=en), observed 2026-10-15, describes campaign parameters and reporting.
Read back the actual setup before claiming collection.
Native apps, private demos and offline actions use their supported attribution path, or retain an explicit measurement gap.
Do not force GA4 or a public website onto every product.

For each metric, retain the exact project/property/account, source, range, timezone, definition and observation time.
Use bounded aggregate data. Exclude credentials, customer identifiers and sensitive audience details from reports and URLs.
Unavailable access needs a bounded export or an unavailable-source label, not a tracking installation under strategy authority.

For X, keep each approved item/revision and eventual post URL/ID joined to its existing campaign destination and outcome definition.
For a thread, retain the individual post IDs and define which observations belong to the experiment before aggregating.
Separate public engagement, owned-post clicks, destination sessions and verified product actions. Do not manufacture missing stages or unique-user totals.
Use the account's authorized readout or a bounded owner export. Publicly visible metrics do not imply unauthenticated API access.
[X metrics](https://docs.x.com/x-api/fundamentals/metrics), checked 2026-10-18, distinguishes public metrics from owned-content metrics requiring user authentication.
It limits non-public, organic and promoted metrics to posts created within the preceding 30 days; refresh this limit before planning collection.
Align the observation window with actual source availability. Preserve unknown clicks and attribution gaps rather than inferring conversions from engagement.
Missing analytics access permits a draft with an explicit measurement gap; it never grants account access or tracking setup.

## Review Results

Compare observations with the prior baseline and declared decision rule.
Distinguish platform clicks, destination sessions and verified product actions; they need not match.
Explain attribution gaps, paid-versus-organic differences and small samples.
An uncontrolled before/after comparison does not prove causal lift.

Return the result, interpretation, confidence, next experiment and product, content or measurement handoffs.
Keep ordinary campaign results in the campaign review. Link deployed product outcomes to their existing release-bound outcome record.
An existing Epic may link the accepted decision and next action; it does not replace the outcome record.
Preserve failed observations and the original hypothesis.
If the window has not elapsed, end the review and name the next useful observation date.
A suggested follow-up is not monitoring or an automation; create one only after an explicit owner request.
