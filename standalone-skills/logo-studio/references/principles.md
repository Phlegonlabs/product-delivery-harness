# Logo Principles

These rules come from published practice. Sources and their check date are at the end.
The project's brief and the owner's decisions take precedence.

## What A Logo Does

A logo identifies. It does not sell, explain or illustrate the service.
A logo takes its meaning from the product behind it, not the other way round.
Do not ask the mark to say what the company does.

## The Three Criteria

Judge every mark against the three criteria of Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv.

- Appropriate: the mark feels right for the field and the brand's character. It is not a picture of the service.
- Distinctive: after one or two looks, a person can describe the mark or draw it from memory.
- Simple: the mark works very small, in one colour, and in every place the brand appears.

Use one idea per mark. When the sentence that describes the mark needs "and", remove a part.

## Choose The Type Of Mark

| Type | Use it when | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wordmark | The name is short and distinctive, and people do not know it yet | Weak as an app icon |
| Combination mark | A new brand needs its name read and a symbol that can later stand alone | Two unrelated parts side by side |
| Lettermark | The name is long, or people already know the brand | An initial says nothing about an unknown brand |
| Pictorial or abstract symbol alone | People already recognize the brand | Vague or anonymous without the name |

For a new brand with a short name, start from a combination mark.
The wordmark does the identifying. The symbol comes from the same idea as the wordmark.

## Common Failures

- A monogram of the initial as the whole idea. Every brand with that letter can use it.
- The first obvious symbol of the field: coin, wallet, arrow, chart, shield, tick, globe, light bulb, leaf, swoosh.
- A literal picture of the product or service.
- A stock typeface with no redrawn letter.
- Detail that fuses or disappears at favicon size.
- An idea taken from the current interface design. The interface changes sooner than the logo.
- A mark that needs its colour to be recognized.
- A style copied from this year's rebrands.

## Wordmark Craft

Choose the typeface for the brand's character, then redraw the letters.
Give the word one memorable feature, not five.
Look for the feature in the name's own letters: a shared diagonal, a joined pair, a cut terminal, a counter.
Set the spacing by eye, letter pair by letter pair.
The word must still read at a glance. Legibility comes before cleverness.
Decide upper case, lower case or mixed case on purpose, from the shapes the letters give.
Confirm that the typeface licence allows a redrawn logo before you outline it.

## Construction

Work in black on white first. Add colour last. Colour must not carry the idea.
A construction grid is a guide. Correct the result by eye.
Round forms overshoot flat forms by about 2 to 4 percent to look the same height.
Thin the strokes at joins and on diagonals, or they look heavier than the stems.
Centre a symbol inside a tile by eye, not by its bounding box.
Use stroke widths that are no thinner than the gaps between them, so the mark holds when small.

## App Icon And Favicon

An app icon carries one concept with one focus point on a simple ground.
Use words in an icon only when the word is the logo.
The platform applies the icon's mask and its dark, tinted or monochrome appearances. Supply full-bleed artwork.
Android adaptive icons use a 108 by 108 container. Keep the symbol inside the centred 66 by 66 safe zone.
Redraw the 16 and 32 pixel favicons on the pixel grid. Do not scale the large symbol down.
Platform rules change. Read the current platform guidance once per round and record the check date.

## Colour And Contrast

Keep one ownable colour or shape constant and let the system around it change.
Logotypes are outside WCAG contrast requirements. Use 3:1 against the ground as the working floor anyway.
A thin or small shape below 3:1 fails. Carry such a mark in the darker colour.
Supply a one-colour black version and a one-colour white version of every mark.
